## The Twin Minerals of Jade

For thousands of years, the word "Jade" was applied to any tough, green stone that could be carved into tools, weapons, and ornaments. 

However, in 1863, French mineralogist Alexis Damour discovered that jade is actually composed of two entirely distinct minerals: **Jadeite** and **Nephrite**.

If you are a collector or [lapidary](/blog/beginners-guide-to-lapidary) artist, knowing the **difference between jadeite and nephrite** is essential for valuation and working.

---

## Mineralogical Comparison Table

Although they look similar to the untrained eye, their chemistry and physical properties are distinct:

| Property | Jadeite | Nephrite |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mineral Group** | Pyroxene | Amphibole |
| **Chemical Formula** | NaAlSi2O6 (Sodium Aluminum Silicate) | Ca2(Mg,Fe)5Si8O22(OH)2 (Calcium Magnesium Silicate) |
| **Crystal Structure** | Interlocking granular crystals | Interlocking fibrous crystals |
| **Mohs Hardness** | 6.5 to 7.0 | 6.0 to 6.5 |
| **Specific Gravity** | 3.25 to 3.35 | 2.90 to 3.03 |
| **Refractive Index** | 1.66 | 1.62 |

---

## 1. Chemical Composition and Mineral Groups

* **Jadeite:** Belongs to the **pyroxene** mineral group. It is rich in sodium and aluminum. Chromium impurities are responsible for the highly prized emerald-green color.
* **Nephrite:** Belongs to the **amphibole** mineral group (specifically the tremolite-actinolite series). It is rich in calcium, magnesium, and iron.

---

## 2. Crystal Structure and Toughness

The most fascinating difference is their internal structure, which dictates how they respond to carving tools:

* **Nephrite's Microscopic Weave:** Nephrite is composed of microcrystalline fibers that are tightly interwoven in a felted, carpet-like weave. This structure makes nephrite incredibly tough, allowing carvers to create thin, interlocking chains without breaking the stone.
* **Jadeite's Granular Mesh:** Jadeite is made of granular, interlocking crystals. While hard, it is more brittle than nephrite and can chip more easily if carved too thin.
